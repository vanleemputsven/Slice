# Why the homepage leads with hours

Someone landing on Slice already pays for Netflix, a gym, or a stream they share. A feature grid does not tell them anything they cannot guess. The sentence they can finish themselves does: a price divided by their own hourly wage is a number of hours.

That figure sits in the first sentence and updates when they change the price or the wage. It uses the same division the app uses for work time. It does not save a subscription and it does not invent one.

The next section shows one shared charge in the three states the list actually has: still ahead, marked inside the reminder lead, and moved forward after it is recorded as charged. The slider after that is the four questions used to add a charge. Both are examples, labelled as such.

The close offers the real next step. A guest answers those questions on `/demo`, which becomes their first list. Someone already signed in opens the overview or adds a charge.

The coral action uses the page's near-black ink. White on this coral is about 3:1, which fails for the button label. The soft coral glow on `body` stays in the app and is cleared while this page is open, so the marketing page is a flat field.
